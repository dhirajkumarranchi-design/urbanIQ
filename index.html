01 — Overview

What is UrbanIQ?

UrbanIQ is a location intelligence tool designed for real estate professionals, developers, urban planners, and informed buyers. Drop a pin anywhere — by address or coordinates — and UrbanIQ systematically searches, categorises, scores, and interprets every relevant amenity within two concentric zones around that point.

The tool produces two proprietary scores — the WorkReach Index for commercial buildings and the NestScore for residential properties — each out of 100, built from four quantitative dimensions. Gemini AI then layers structured insights, gap analysis, and persona-specific commentary on top of the raw data.

⬡ Core Premise

Location quality is not binary. It is a function of what exists nearby, how close it is, how good it is, and how many options you have. UrbanIQ measures all four — separately and together.

02 — Zone System

The Two-Zone Model

UrbanIQ places two concentric circles around the pinned location. The user controls both radii independently using sliders — Zone 1 from 500m to 4.9km, Zone 2 always larger than Zone 1, up to 5km.

Zone 1
Inner circle. The primary daily-life radius. Amenities assigned here (transit, cafes, pharmacies, schools) are expected to be accessible on foot or a short commute. Slider range: 500m – 4.9km.
Zone 2
Outer circle. The extended-reach radius. Amenities assigned here (hospitals, universities, shopping malls) are expected to be reachable by vehicle or transit. Always larger than Zone 1. Slider range: Zone 1 + 100m up to 5km.
Search Strategy
Every amenity type is searched once at Zone 2's radius. Results are then split by actual measured distance — places within Zone 1's radius are attributed to Zone 1; places between Zone 1 and Zone 2 are attributed to Zone 2. This prevents missed results.
Primary Zone
Each amenity type has a "primary zone" designation indicating where it is most expected. This affects scoring weights but does not limit where the app will search. A pharmacy found in Zone 2 still counts — it's just weighted as a proximity gap.

WorkReach Index — Commercial Buildings

Designed for office buildings, co-working spaces, and commercial real estate. Measures a location's fitness for business operations — client accessibility, employee convenience, and operational infrastructure.

Primary amenity set: Transit stops, parking, ATMs/banks, restaurants, cafés, pharmacies, convenience stores, police (Zone 1); hospitals, hotels, fuel stations, shopping malls, gyms (Zone 2).

NestScore — Residential Complexes

Designed for housing projects, apartments, and residential townships. Measures a location's day-to-day liveability — how well the surrounding area supports families, daily routines, and quality of life.

Primary amenity set: Grocery stores, schools, parks, pharmacies, transit, police, places of worship (Zone 1); hospitals, universities, malls, gyms, petrol stations, cinemas, libraries, vet clinics (Zone 2).

Score Bands

🟢 Excellent 85 – 100

Exceptional location quality. The area is well-served across all amenity types, with quality options close by. Suitable for premium positioning.

🟡 Good 65 – 84

🟠 Average 45 – 64

🔴 Poor 0 – 44

Significant infrastructure deficit. Multiple critical amenities are absent or unrated. Not recommended without planned infrastructure improvements.

04 — Methodology

How the Score is Calculated

The score is a weighted composite of four independent dimensions. Each dimension is calculated as a ratio from 0–100, then multiplied by its weight. The four weighted values are summed to produce the final score, capped at 100.

⬡ Formula

Score = (Coverage × Wc) + (Proximity × Wp) + (Quality × Wq) + (Density × Wd)
where W values are weights expressed as percentages totalling 100.

Dimension Office Wt. Residential Wt. How It's Measured
Coverage 35% 30% Percentage of expected amenity types for which at least one place was found anywhere in either zone. A missing amenity type is a zero-weighted gap. Formula: (types found ÷ total expected types) × weight.
Proximity 25% 30% For each amenity type with a quality option, measures how close the nearest quality place is, scaled linearly against its primary zone's radius. Closer = higher score. Formula: (1 − distance ÷ maxRadius) per type, weighted average.
Quality 25% 25% Percentage of amenity types for which at least one "quality" place exists — defined as rating ≥ 3.8 stars with ≥ 10 Google reviews. A type with places but no quality option scores zero on this dimension. Formula: (types with quality option ÷ total expected types) × weight.
Density 15% 15% Average count of places per amenity type, capped at 5 to prevent over-rewarding saturated categories like restaurants in city centres. Formula: (min(count, 5) ÷ 5) per type, weighted average.

Why the Weights Differ

Residential scoring shifts 5 percentage points from Coverage into Proximity because how far you walk to a school or pharmacy matters more than whether a fourth mall exists. Office scoring prioritises Coverage because for commercial tenants, presence of varied amenity types (hotels for clients, transit for staff) is the primary concern.

Quality Threshold Rationale

The quality threshold of ≥ 3.8 stars with ≥ 10 reviews is calibrated to filter out low-quality or unverified establishments without being too restrictive. A 4.0-star threshold would eliminate many excellent but modestly reviewed local businesses in smaller cities. A 10-review minimum prevents a single outlier review from skewing results.

Per-Amenity Card Metrics

Total Found
All Google Places results of the given type within the zone radius, regardless of rating or review count.
Quality Count
Count of places meeting the quality threshold: rating ≥ 3.8 and user_ratings_total ≥ 10. This is the quality-verified subset.
Closest Quality
Among quality-threshold places in a zone, the one with the shortest straight-line distance from the target pin. This is the recommended default option.
Best Rated
Among quality-threshold places in a zone, the one with the highest star rating. Shown separately when it differs meaningfully from Closest Quality — indicating a trade-off between convenience and excellence.
Fallback Marker
When no quality-threshold place exists in a zone, the absolute closest place (any rating) is placed on the map with a dashed border to signal it has not been quality-verified.

Data Source

All amenity data is sourced from the Google Places API (nearbySearch endpoint). Each amenity type is a direct Google Places type= string (e.g. hospital, transit_station). Distances are calculated using the Haversine formula on returned geometry.location coordinates. No third-party data, scraped data, or pre-cached datasets are used.

06 — AI Features

Gemini AI Integration

UrbanIQ integrates Gemini 2.5 Flash at five specific points in the analysis pipeline. Each AI feature is independently triggered and gracefully skipped if no Gemini key is provided. The raw data analysis runs entirely on Google Places without Gemini.

⬡ Model

Gemini 2.5 Flash is used throughout. Thinking mode is explicitly disabled (thinkingBudget: 0) to ensure fast, direct responses. Temperature is set to 0.2 for consistent, fact-anchored outputs.

1
Natural Language Amenity Search
Type any amenity in plain language — "creche", "dialysis centre", "dosa restaurant" — and Gemini maps it to the correct Google Places type string plus an optional keyword. This makes the amenity list open-ended beyond the built-in 15 types. Gemini returns a structured JSON object with type, keyword, display label, and zone assignment.
2
Persona-Based Score Reweighting
When a persona is selected (Family, Young Professional, Elderly, Corporate HQ, Startup), Gemini's persona configuration modifies the scoring weight multipliers per amenity type — without new API calls. A Family persona doubles the weight of schools and parks; an Elderly persona doubles hospitals and pharmacies. The score ring recalculates instantly.
3
Gap Analysis — Structured Insight Report
After all Places results are in, Gemini receives the score breakdown plus lists of missing and no-quality amenity types. It returns a structured JSON with three specific insights, three key takeaway chips, and a one-sentence verdict. Rendered as a numbered list with a verdict block.
4
Persona Fit Analysis
When a persona is active, a second Gemini call generates three persona-specific insights (one general fit assessment, one strength, one weakness), three takeaway chips, and a fit verdict. Uses blue accent to visually distinguish it from the green gap analysis block.
5
Per-Amenity Closest vs. Best Advice
For amenity types where "closest quality" and "best rated" are meaningfully different (distance gap > 300m and rating gap > 0.2 stars), Gemini generates a single 15-word advisory sentence — "Worth the extra 1.2km for the Apollo rating vs the closer clinic." Injected directly into each amenity card.

07 — Analysis Flow

Step-by-Step: What Happens When You Click Analyse

1
Location Resolution

The input (address string or lat/long) is sent to Google Geocoding API. For lat/long inputs, a reverse geocode and a 120m-radius nearby places search run in parallel to auto-populate the pin label.

2
Amenity Config Selection

Based on building type (office / residential), the correct amenity config is loaded — 13 built-in types plus any custom Gemini-mapped types. Each has a Google Places type string, a weight, and a primary zone.

3
Parallel Places Search

All amenity types are searched simultaneously against the Google Places nearbySearch API at Zone 2's radius, with a 170ms stagger between calls. Each call returns up to 20 results (Google's limit per request).

4
Distance Calculation & Zone Split

For each returned place, Haversine distance is calculated from the pin. Results are then split into Zone 1 (≤ c1) and Zone 2 (c1 < d ≤ c2). Within each zone, quality places are filtered (≥ 3.8★, ≥ 10 reviews), sorted by distance and by rating.

5
Progressive Card Rendering

As each amenity type's results arrive, its card updates in real time — from skeleton loading state to populated data. The progress bar tracks completion.

6
Map Marker Placement

All quality places in both zones receive numbered SVG markers in their category colour. If no quality place exists in a zone, the closest place gets a dashed-border fallback marker. The map legend is built from the active amenity config.

7
Score Calculation

Coverage, Proximity, Quality, and Density are calculated across all amenity types with active persona weight modifiers applied. The four weighted values are summed into the final score and animated into the ring gauge.

8
AI Insights (if Gemini key set)

Gap analysis and persona insights fire concurrently as background calls. Per-amenity closest-vs-best advice fires as a single batched Gemini call for all eligible amenity types. Results render as they return.

08 — Setup Guide

Getting Started

UrbanIQ is a single .html file. Open it in any modern browser. No installation, server, or build step required. Two API keys are needed.

01

Google Maps API Key

Required. Enables map rendering, geocoding, and Places search. In Google Cloud Console, enable: Maps JavaScript API, Places API, and Geocoding API. Restrict the key to your domain in production.

02

Gemini API Key

Optional. Enables all five AI features. Obtain from Google AI Studio (aistudio.google.com). The free tier is sufficient for personal use. Without it, all Places analysis and scoring still work.

03

Enter & Test Keys

Expand the API Configuration panel at the top of the sidebar. Paste each key and click TEST. A real API call is made to verify connectivity before the key is accepted.

04

Pin a Location & Analyse

Select building type, enter an address or coordinates, adjust the zone sliders, optionally select a persona, then click Analyse Location. Results appear progressively as Places data returns.

⚠ Security Note

API keys entered in the app are held in browser memory only for the current session. They are never transmitted to any server other than Google's own APIs. For shared or public deployments, use API key restrictions (HTTP referrer restrictions) in Google Cloud Console.

09 — Glossary

Terms & Definitions

WorkReach Index
The proprietary location score (0–100) for commercial and office buildings. Measures business-relevant infrastructure density, quality, and access.
NestScore
The proprietary location score (0–100) for residential complexes. Measures liveability, daily-life convenience, and quality of nearby services.
Quality Threshold
The minimum bar a place must clear to count as "quality": Google rating ≥ 3.8 stars AND user_ratings_total ≥ 10 reviews.
Coverage Score
The fraction of expected amenity types for which at least one place was found. A type with zero results is an unmet need.
Proximity Score
A measure of how close the nearest quality place of each type is, relative to the primary zone's expected radius.
Quality Score
The fraction of amenity types for which at least one quality-threshold place exists anywhere in either zone.
Density Score
Average number of places per amenity type, capped at 5 per type to prevent over-rewarding saturated categories.
Primary Zone
The zone where an amenity type is expected to be most relevant. Affects scoring weight but not search scope — all types are searched in both zones.
Haversine Distance
Great-circle distance between two lat/long coordinates. Used for all distance calculations to account for Earth's curvature at neighbourhood scale.
Fallback Marker
A map marker placed for the closest available place when no quality-threshold place exists in a zone. Displayed with a dashed border and reduced opacity.
Closest Quality
The nearest quality-threshold place for a given amenity type in a given zone. The primary recommended option.
Best Rated
The highest-rated quality-threshold place in a zone. Shown when it meaningfully differs from Closest Quality, indicating a proximity-versus-excellence trade-off.
Persona Reweighting
Score weight multipliers applied per amenity type based on selected user profile. Does not trigger new API calls — recalculates from cached scan data.
nearbySearch
The Google Places API endpoint used for all amenity searches. Returns up to 20 results per call within a specified radius and type.
thinkingBudget: 0
Gemini 2.5 Flash configuration that disables internal chain-of-thought reasoning, ensuring fast direct responses within the token budget.
Gap Analysis
An AI-generated report identifying amenity types with zero results or no quality options, interpreted in the context of the building type and score.
